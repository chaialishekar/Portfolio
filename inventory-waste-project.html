Case study — operations analytics

The same network was over-stocked and under-stocked at once.

A data model built to show why "reduce excess inventory" and "avoid stockouts" usually fail as separate initiatives — because in this network, they were the same failure, happening on the same days.

01 — The question

Why do "cut waste" and "protect service level" projects keep working against each other?

Most inventory dashboards report one number: overstock or stockout rate. Reported separately, each looks like a normal, manageable tail. Reported together, against the same store and the same product on the same day, they show something different — a reorder policy that was set once, for average conditions, and never revisited per SKU or per location as those conditions diverged.

03 — Method

Built as a star schema, not a flat export

Data model

One fact table (Fact_Inventory) at daily store × product grain, joined to Dim_Date, Dim_Product, and Dim_Store — the standard shape for a Power BI-ready analytical model, not a single wide spreadsheet.

Core metric

Days-of-supply per store/product, from a trailing 28-day rolling demand average. A documented 3-day target buffer defines "excess" — a stated business rule, not a fact pulled from the source data, and called out explicitly rather than buried in a formula.

KPI layer

DAX measures for total excess value, % of store-days over target, % of store-days under 1 day of supply, and month-over-month movement — designed to sit side by side on one page rather than live in separate reports.

Source

Public retail inventory dataset (73K+ rows), used here to demonstrate the modeling and analysis approach — figures are illustrative of the method, not a real company's operating numbers.
